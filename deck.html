TL;DR

The app treats every restock like a first visit. We turn it into a loop that is already running.

Problem

Once a month Ramesh rebuilds a long list from memory, reads it out on calls, pays a large amount and has no list to check what arrives.

Insight · assumption

A restock is not one list. Every item runs out at its own speed, and the need shows up during the month, not on order day.

Solution

A loop of four moments: capture needs all month, compose a Due Now basket, confirm in one cart, check on arrival. Kari, an AI helper, runs each step. Ramesh makes the final tap.

How we will know

Time to place a restock, order lines captured before order day, fewer mid-month emergency orders, missing items reported.

The ask

Approve a four step validation: shop visits, a voice test, a click-through test with this prototype, then a one district pilot.

The idea

Restocking is a loop with four moments, not a list

Every item runs out at its own speed, and the need appears mid-month when a customer asks. So the design follows the month, not the order form.

01 · CAPTURE

Catch the need

All month, in seconds
  • Yaad List: note it by tap, voice or photo
  • Lost-sale tap: "customer asked, I had none"
  • Kari listens on a call
02 · COMPOSE

Build the basket

Order day, mostly done for him
  • Due Now basket: only what is running out
  • My Bundles: a set in one tap
  • Kari suggests, with a reason
03 · CONFIRM

Decide and pay

One cart, one tap
  • Cart: fill-up nudge, budget, schemes
  • Kari double-checks
  • Khata, UPI or cash
04 · CHECK

Receive and learn

Next day, then it repeats
  • Track, and add more before dispatch
  • Goods Check by list or bill photo
  • His rhythm updates for next time

The rule across all four: AI does the work, Ramesh makes the call. Kari never places an order.

UI and UX principles

Eight rules every screen was checked against

1

One primary action

Each screen has a single orange button. Everything else is outlined or a link.

2

Recognition over recall

Real pack photos, last quantity and last order date. He never has to remember.

3

Numbers before words

Total, price and margin lead. Sentences are short and optional.

4

Progressive disclosure

Suggestions and deals stay folded until asked for. Home stays calm.

5

Forgiving by default

Steppers, a reset, a 2 minute edit window, and Kari never places the order.

6

Visible system status

Chips say what is applied. Tracking has four plain steps. Every tap answers back.

7

Consistency

Price pill, add button and header match the live app, so nothing must be relearned.

8

Access for everyone

Hindi script and English, explain and listen buttons, voice and camera input, dark text on light.

Language · the user decides the design

For this user, language is the product

Ramesh reads Hindi first and little English. Sunita is new to ordering apps. If a word is not understood, the feature behind it does not exist.

Proper Hindi, proper script

Every screen is written in Devanagari and set in Noto Sans Devanagari, with taller line height and no letter spacing so conjuncts stay intact.

English, one tap away

Both languages are complete. A new user picks on the first screen; anyone can switch later.

A "?" that explains

Margin, bulk price, khata, wallet and Kari each open a two line explanation in plain words.

"सुनें" · Listen

Every explanation can be read aloud, for anyone who would rather hear than read.

Words he already uses stay as they are: ऑर्डर, बिल, स्कीम, मार्जिन. Numbers stay in Western digits, as on his price tags and in the live app. The Hindi was written from a glossary and needs a native writer's review.

Yaad List: catch the need the moment it appears

  • One tap, any day. "+ Add a note" opens a sheet: type, pick, or say it to Kari.

  • It shows its source. Each line says "Customer asked · 18 Nov" or "You said it".

  • Budget overflow lands here too. Items held back are kept for next time.

Live · Kari across the loop

Kari is a phone call, because that is how he orders today

  • Speaks his way. "Atta 8 bori kar do aur 2 peti sarson tel" is understood as written.
  • A slip, with pictures. Every item Kari heard appears with its photo and a stepper to correct it.
  • It asks, it does not assume. "Tata Tea Gold stock mein nahi hai, Red Label rakh doon?"
  • It nudges once. On "bas", Kari mentions anything he usually takes but left out.
  • It answers. "Toor dal ka bhav kya hai?" and "Mera order kahan hai?"

Live · 03 Confirm

One cart, and credit that works like his distributor's ledger

  • A real cart. Count in the header; edit, empty, or save as a bundle. A green bar always shows what he saves and his margin.
  • Fill-up Nudge and Budget Mode. "Take 4 more and save ₹100", or set a budget and Kari holds back the least urgent items.
  • Kari double-checks. Compares with last month, checks the khata limit, offers forgotten items and schemes.
  • Put it on khata. Default payment for a returning shop, settled once at month end.
  • UPI and cash stay. A new shop starts with these; khata unlocks after the first order.
  • After the tap: an ETA, two minutes to edit, and a WhatsApp copy.

Risks

What could go wrong, and the fallback

A suggestion is wrong

Every suggestion shows its reason and source, is never applied silently, and can be declined in one tap.

Kari mishears or misreads

Pictures confirm each item. Search and browse always work without Kari.

The list goes stale

Quantities are editable in place, and the list rebuilds from every order.

Khata is not approved

The flow still works with UPI and cash. Khata is one option on one screen.

Breaking distributor trust

Pilot with distributors already on the platform; do not replace the relationship in the flow.

Needs a business decision

AI running cost, lending partner and limits, ETA data from logistics, storing order history as a list.
